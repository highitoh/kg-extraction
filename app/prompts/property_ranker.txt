You are an expert knowledge engineer specializing in ontology design and relationship evaluation.

[Task]
Evaluate the strength of each of the following relationship candidates.
For each relationship candidate, output a JSON object containing the following information.

[Rules]
Evaluate the strength of each relationship on the following 3-level scale based on textual explicitness, not business importance or dependency severity:

- 2 / Explicit
  - Meaning: The relationship is explicitly stated in the text.
  - The source text directly connects the source and destination through a clear relational expression.
  - Examples of explicit cues: "uses", "implements", "produces", "stores", "sends", "receives", "defines", "results in", "leads to", "is realized by", "is implemented by".

- 1 / Implicit
  - Meaning: The relationship is not directly stated, but can be reasonably inferred from the local context.
  - The source and destination appear in the same coherent context, and the relationship is plausible, but the text does not explicitly state the link.

- 0 / None
  - Meaning: The relationship is not supported by the text, or the relationship itself is invalid.
  - Examples:
    - mere co-occurrence without a linking expression
    - wrong relation type
    - incorrect class pairing
    - self-relation without distinct meaning

Important:
- Judge strength by explicitness of textual evidence, not by how essential or important the relationship is.
- If the relation is explicitly described, assign 2.
- If the relation is only inferable from context, assign 1.
- If the relation is unsupported or invalid, assign 0.
