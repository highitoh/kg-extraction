[Task]
Work in this order.

Step 1 - Decide the groups.
Read the source text of each member and keep together only those that refer to the same
thing. Labels that merely share wording, or that name related but distinct things, belong
in separate groups. The proposal you were given may be wrong; splitting it into several
groups, or into one group per member, is a correct answer. Decide this before you think
about names.

Step 2 - Name each group.
For each group you decided in step 1, choose its canonical label from the labels in that
group. Weigh each qualifier a label carries against what the source text takes for granted:
leave out what the reader already knows, keep what the reader would otherwise have to
guess. A document written about one product need not repeat that product's name in the
label; but where it describes several things of the same kind, the label has to say which
one this is. When the document establishes a short form for a term, prefer that short form
over the full formal name.
